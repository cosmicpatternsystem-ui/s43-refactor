# Commercial Sovereignty & Revenue Protection Doctrine

## Status
Approved hardening doctrine for commercial governance, revenue acceptance, authority discipline, and downside containment.

## Purpose
Define the top-level commercial doctrine for ASO-X so that revenue is accepted only when it is collectible, margin-safe, contractually bounded, operationally supportable, and durably auditable.

## Core Doctrine
Revenue is not accepted as success unless it is collectible, margin-safe, contractually bounded, operationally supportable, and durably auditable.

## Strategic Requirements

### 1. Commercial sovereignty
Commercial commitments must remain subordinate to repository policy, durable governance controls, and explicit authority boundaries.

### 2. Revenue protection
No revenue path may be treated as valid if it creates uncontrolled liability, unbounded support burden, hidden concession debt, or unacceptable cash exposure.

### 3. Durable accountability
Every high-impact commercial decision must be attributable to a decision owner, a policy basis, an approval path, and an immutable audit trail.

### 4. Bounded obligation
Commercial approvals must explicitly identify contractual obligations, service expectations, liability edges, and termination or rollback paths.

### 5. Delivery realism
No commercial approval is valid unless the committed outcome is operationally supportable by the delivery system that must honor it.

## Mandatory control gates

### 1. Collectability gate
Commercial acceptance must assess whether revenue is realistically collectible under the agreed payment posture and counterparty conditions.

### 2. Margin protection gate
Commercial acceptance must preserve defined margin floors, cost coverage expectations, and downside resilience.

### 3. Authority gate
Commercial acceptance must conform to the commercial authority matrix and any higher-order governance constraints.

### 4. Obligation gate
Commercial acceptance must identify refunds, penalties, credits, service levels, exclusivity, indemnity, and any long-tail obligation risk.

### 5. Operational feasibility gate
Commercial acceptance must verify that delivery, support, billing, and governance functions can sustain the commitment.

### 6. Exception governance gate
Any exception to policy, pricing, terms, commitments, or support posture must be time-bounded, explicitly approved, justified, and recorded.

### 7. Audit durability gate
Commercial approvals must be recorded in durable artifacts suitable for long-horizon audit, compliance review, and institutional memory.

## Required decision record
Every high-impact commercial decision must capture:
- decision owner
- approving authority
- policy basis
- counterparty context
- collectability posture
- pricing and margin posture
- contractual obligations
- operational delivery basis
- risk classification
- exception status
- rollback or termination path
- durable record location

## Prohibited patterns
The following patterns are prohibited:
- verbal-only commercial commitments
- single-actor approval for high-impact exceptions
- price or term deviations without authority basis
- margin-destructive concessions without escalated approval
- open-ended obligations without bounded language
- undocumented commercial exceptions
- revenue acceptance without delivery supportability

## Enforcement expectations
Repository governance artifacts, approval workflows, and hardening tests should treat this doctrine as the parent commercial policy layer for real-money decision control.

## Acceptance criteria
This doctrine is acceptable only if:
- revenue quality is prioritized over nominal revenue volume
- uncontrolled liability is rejected
- policy-authorized approvals are enforced
- commercial exceptions are bounded and auditable
- operationally unsupported commitments are blocked
- durable evidence exists for material approvals
