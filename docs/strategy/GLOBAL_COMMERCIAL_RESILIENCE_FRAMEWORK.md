# Global Commercial Resilience Framework

## Status
Canonical.
Normative.
Repository-governed.
Commercially binding for proposal, pricing, discounting, payment, delivery commitment, renewal, escalation, exception handling, and counterparty acceptance pathways.

## Purpose
Define an enterprise-grade commercial resilience framework that protects gross margin, cash realization, delivery credibility, operating capacity, and contractual survivability under real-money conditions.

## Core Principles
1. Revenue quality outranks headline revenue.
2. Cash certainty outranks booked optimism.
3. Delivery truth outranks sales enthusiasm.
4. Margin discipline outranks discretionary discounting.
5. Counterparty fitness outranks logo acquisition.
6. Written approval outranks verbal assurance.
7. Repository authority outranks ad hoc exception making.
8. Repeatable controls outrank heroics.
9. Measurable accountability outranks ambiguity.
10. Survival-grade prudence outranks vanity growth.

## Commercial Non-Negotiables
- No commitment may be made without delivery plausibility.
- No pricing may be issued below the approved floor without recorded authority.
- No payment term may be offered that materially increases collection risk without recorded approval.
- No concession may be granted unless the give/get exchange is explicit.
- No bespoke obligation may be accepted without identified owner, cost, and operating impact.
- No renewal may be assumed healthy without usage, payment, support, and risk review.
- No urgent deal may bypass controls solely because it is urgent.
- No sales promise may override repository-governed delivery constraints.
- No customer pressure may redefine acceptance criteria after commitment without controlled change.
- No commercial exception is valid if it is not documented.

## Revenue Quality Guardrails
### Approved Revenue Conditions
Revenue pursuit is preferred only where all of the following are true:
- The scope is sufficiently defined.
- The delivery path is plausible with current or approved capacity.
- Pricing preserves strategic and financial viability.
- Payment terms do not create unacceptable working-capital stress.
- Legal and operational obligations are understood.
- Counterparty conduct is compatible with disciplined execution.

### Revenue Degradation Signals
The following degrade deal quality and trigger elevated scrutiny:
- Extreme discount pressure.
- Unfunded custom work.
- Vague or shifting scope.
- Multi-party decision ambiguity.
- Deferred payment preference.
- Procurement asymmetry.
- Artificial deadlines.
- Demands for success guarantees outside controllable boundaries.
- Unpriced support load.
- Hidden integration complexity.
- Renewal dependency disguised as initial opportunity quality.

## Pricing Discipline
### Pricing Authority Bands
1. Standard Terms Band  
   Standard pricing, standard payment terms, standard commitments, no extraordinary concession.
2. Managed Flexibility Band  
   Limited deviation allowed with explicit recorded justification and accountable owner.
3. Controlled Exception Band  
   Material pricing or term deviation requires cross-functional recorded approval.
4. Executive Exception Band  
   Atypical strategic exception requires explicit senior commercial authority and risk acknowledgment.
5. NO-GO Band  
   Deal may not proceed.

### Discount Rules
- Discounting must be justified by measurable value exchange.
- Volume claims must be evidenced before value is granted.
- Multi-year concessions require enforceable commitment structure.
- Strategic logo rationale alone does not justify permanent margin destruction.
- Time pressure is not a discount rationale.
- Competitive pressure is not sufficient rationale without strategic analysis.
- Discount stacking is prohibited unless explicitly approved.
- Hidden concessions are prohibited.
- Side-letter economics that undercut repository-governed pricing are prohibited.
- Any concession must identify expiry condition.

### Margin Protection
- Gross margin floor must be preserved unless a higher authority explicitly approves otherwise.
- Services, support, integration, and risk-bearing obligations must be costed before quote release.
- Unbounded revisions, custom reports, bespoke integrations, and premium response commitments must never be treated as free.
- Commercial packaging must reflect total lifecycle delivery load, not only initial close probability.

## Payment and Cash Realization
### Payment Term Defaults
- Preference: upfront, milestone-based, or short-cycle invoicing.
- Extended payment terms require explicit risk acknowledgment.
- Acceptance-linked payment structures require precise acceptance language.
- Any material back-loaded cash structure requires elevated approval.
- Collection friction risk must be assessed before signature.

### Payment Risk Indicators
- Chronic late payment pattern.
- Procurement opacity.
- Budget uncertainty.
- Dependency on third-party release events.
- Informal promises replacing contractual clarity.
- Requests for broad acceptance discretion.
- Resistance to milestone billing.
- Demand for delivery before commercial finalization.

### Cash Preservation Rules
- The organization must protect liquidity before symbolic revenue.
- Deals with attractive headline value but adverse cash characteristics require high skepticism.
- Non-payment exposure, refund asymmetry, or delayed realization must be treated as core commercial risk.
- Revenue that destabilizes cash operations is commercially defective.

## Commitment Integrity
### Promise Control
- Proposal language must align with actual delivery capability.
- Demonstration environments must not be represented as production guarantees.
- Future roadmap concepts must not be sold as committed deliverables unless explicitly approved.
- Turnaround promises must reflect staffed and sustainable operating reality.
- Response commitments, service levels, data obligations, and integration outcomes must be stated in bounded language.

### Change Discipline
- Scope expansion after pricing requires formal reassessment.
- Customer-originated ambiguity must not be absorbed silently.
- Any material change in assumptions must trigger quote review.
- Commercial artifacts must remain traceable to approved repository language.

## Counterparty Fitness
### Positive Signals
- Timely engagement.
- Defined decision process.
- Clear budget authority.
- Operationally credible stakeholders.
- Fair exchange orientation.
- Willingness to document commitments.

### Negative Signals
- Chronic ambiguity.
- Demands for unlimited flexibility.
- Pressure for informal side agreements.
- Requests to bypass approval structure.
- Pattern of extracting unpaid work.
- Refusal to clarify acceptance or payment mechanics.
- High reputational signal but poor operating discipline.
- Repeated repricing after substantive effort.

### NO-GO Counterparty Triggers
- Probable non-payment risk.
- Unacceptable legal or procurement asymmetry.
- Demand for impossible delivery commitments.
- Requirement for uncapped support or liability posture outside approved framework.
- Coercive process behavior that prevents controlled execution.
- Material dishonesty or documented bad faith.

## Exception Governance
### Exception Preconditions
An exception may be considered only if:
- The business rationale is explicit.
- The downside is bounded.
- The owner is named.
- The concession is reversible, time-limited, or compensated.
- The approval path is recorded.
- The exception does not create silent precedent.

### Exception Record Requirements
Each exception record must identify:
- Opportunity or account reference.
- Requested deviation.
- Baseline rule being deviated from.
- Commercial rationale.
- Financial impact.
- Delivery impact.
- Cash-flow impact.
- Risk owner.
- Approvers.
- Expiry or review date.
- Reuse prohibition unless reapproved.

## Deal Review Triggers
Mandatory review is required if any of the following apply:
- Price below standard threshold.
- Margin compression beyond approved band.
- Non-standard payment terms.
- Material services or integration exposure.
- Novel contractual structure.
- Customer-controlled acceptance ambiguity.
- Executive pressure for accelerated close.
- Large logo pursuit with poor give/get balance.
- Renewal at degraded economics.
- Any element classified as reputationally attractive but operationally hazardous.

## Renewal Protection
- Renewal must be earned by usage, value realization, support health, and payment performance.
- Temporary launch concessions must not silently convert into permanent economic weakness.
- Renewal quoting must re-evaluate support burden, scope creep, inflationary cost, and service intensity.
- Customer familiarity is not a substitute for commercial rigor.

## Global Operating Doctrine
- Commercial doctrine must be portable across geographies while allowing lawful local implementation.
- Local practice may adapt execution details but may not weaken global control intent.
- High-pressure market conditions do not suspend resilience rules.
- Every region must preserve pricing integrity, cash discipline, and delivery truth.

## Anti-Fragility Controls
- The organization must learn from every damaged deal, not merely celebrate closed deals.
- Loss reviews are mandatory for failed or rejected opportunities with high effort or high strategic relevance.
- Win reviews are mandatory where economics are weak despite closure.
- Repeated exception patterns must trigger baseline policy recalibration.
- Commercial risk telemetry must be tracked as a first-class operating signal.

## Minimum Evidence Standard
No commercially material approval is complete unless supported by durable written evidence sufficient for later review by operators, finance, governance, and successor decision-makers.

## Enforcement
- This document governs commercial resilience expectations across pricing, terms, concessions, and commitments.
- Linked tests define minimum repository presence and structural integrity requirements.
- Any future weakening change must be explicit, reviewable, and justified through standard repository controls.
