# Commercial Authority Matrix

This document defines the commercial authority model for ASO-X.

It converts commercial doctrine and hardening requirements into explicit decision rights, escalation boundaries, failure conditions, and repo-governed operating rules.

## Purpose

The Commercial Authority Matrix exists to prevent informal, inconsistent, or margin-destructive commercial decisions.

It establishes who may authorize commercial concessions, under what constraints, and when a deal must be escalated, rejected, or restructured.

Commercial authority is treated as a controlled operating surface, not a personal discretion layer.

## Scope

This matrix applies to:

- Pricing exceptions.
- Discounts.
- Payment terms.
- Contractual concessions.
- Service-level commitments.
- Delivery promises.
- Data handling commitments.
- Retention, audit, and evidence obligations.
- Strategic account exceptions.
- Any agreement that can affect margin, risk, durability, reputation, or operational load.

## Non-Negotiable Principles

The following principles are mandatory:

1. No commercial promise may bypass repo-governed policy.
2. No discount may destroy the minimum acceptable margin floor.
3. No sales urgency may override governance, security, retention, or delivery reality.
4. No custom commitment may be made without explicit implementation ownership.
5. No exception is valid unless it is documented, reviewable, and traceable.
6. No verbal commercial exception is authoritative.
7. No customer-specific concession may silently become a platform obligation.
8. No one may trade long-term operating durability for short-term revenue.

## Authority Levels

### Level 0: Standard Commercial Motion

Permitted without escalation when all of the following are true:

- Standard pricing applies.
- Standard payment terms apply.
- Standard delivery terms apply.
- Standard support terms apply.
- Standard legal terms apply.
- No custom data, retention, security, or integration promise is introduced.
- No engineering, operations, finance, or governance exception is required.

### Level 1: Minor Commercial Exception

Permitted only when the exception is documented and does not affect strategic durability.

Examples:

- Small pricing adjustment within approved discount bounds.
- Minor invoicing accommodation.
- Non-material contract wording clarification.
- Standard implementation timeline adjustment with confirmed delivery capacity.

Level 1 exceptions must not introduce:

- New technical obligations.
- New security obligations.
- New retention obligations.
- New uptime commitments.
- New audit commitments.
- Margin degradation below the approved floor.

### Level 2: Material Commercial Exception

Requires explicit approval before commitment.

Examples:

- Non-standard discounting.
- Extended payment terms.
- Customer-specific delivery sequencing.
- Custom reporting commitments.
- Custom onboarding obligations.
- Non-standard support expectations.
- Commercial concessions that affect delivery, support, or cash timing.

Level 2 exceptions require documented rationale and owner acknowledgment.

### Level 3: Strategic or High-Risk Commercial Exception

Requires senior review and may require a NO-GO decision.

Examples:

- Below-floor margin.
- Unfunded implementation work.
- Customer-specific product roadmap commitment.
- Non-standard security promise.
- Non-standard data residency, retention, deletion, or audit commitment.
- Revenue recognition ambiguity.
- Contractual penalties or liquidated damages.
- Exclusivity.
- Most-favored-nation pricing.
- Unlimited liability.
- Unbounded support obligation.
- Any term that creates systemic risk.

Level 3 exceptions must be treated as controlled risk events.

## Discount Authority

Discounts are governed by value preservation, margin protection, and strategic consistency.

### Allowed Discount Conditions

A discount may be considered only when at least one of the following is true:

- Deal size materially improves economic efficiency.
- Multi-year commitment improves revenue durability.
- Payment terms improve cash reliability.
- Strategic account value is documented.
- Scope is reduced proportionally.
- Delivery cost is materially lower than standard assumptions.

### Disallowed Discount Conditions

Discounts must not be granted because of:

- Sales pressure alone.
- Competitive fear without documented strategy.
- Customer preference for lower price without reciprocal value.
- End-of-period urgency.
- Unverified future expansion claims.
- Desire to avoid a difficult negotiation.
- Internal lack of confidence in value.

### Discount Guardrails

Every non-standard discount must preserve:

- Margin floor.
- Delivery feasibility.
- Support feasibility.
- Contractual clarity.
- Renewal integrity.
- Pricing precedent control.

A discount without documented rationale is invalid.

## Margin Floor

The margin floor is a hard commercial boundary.

A deal must be escalated when:

- Gross margin falls below the approved minimum.
- Implementation cost is uncertain.
- Support cost is likely to exceed standard assumptions.
- Custom work is not separately priced.
- Payment terms create cash exposure.
- Concessions create hidden operational cost.

Below-floor deals are not commercial wins unless explicitly approved as strategic investments.

## Payment Terms Authority

Standard payment terms are preferred.

Non-standard payment terms require escalation when they include:

- Delayed payment.
- Usage-based uncertainty without protections.
- Customer-controlled acceptance triggers.
- Long collection exposure.
- Milestone ambiguity.
- Cancellation asymmetry.
- Refund or clawback exposure.

Payment terms must not convert revenue into unpriced financing.

## Contractual Concessions

The following contractual concessions require explicit approval:

- Unlimited liability.
- Broad indemnity.
- Customer-controlled termination for convenience.
- Unbounded service credits.
- Penalty clauses.
- Exclusivity.
- Most-favored-nation clauses.
- Non-standard audit rights.
- Non-standard data processing obligations.
- Non-standard retention or deletion obligations.
- Non-standard regulatory representations.
- Assignment restrictions that impair durability.

Any concession that creates open-ended exposure must be treated as Level 3.

## Delivery Commitments

No delivery commitment may be made unless:

- Scope is clear.
- Owner is identified.
- Timeline is feasible.
- Dependencies are documented.
- Acceptance criteria are defined.
- Support load is understood.
- Implementation cost is priced or approved.

The following are prohibited without escalation:

- Roadmap promises.
- Custom feature commitments.
- Guaranteed delivery dates without delivery owner approval.
- Commitments dependent on unbuilt capabilities.
- Commitments that require unsafe concurrent edits.
- Commitments that weaken artifact retention or governance controls.

## Security, Data, and Retention Commitments

Security, data, and retention commitments are commercial risk surfaces.

The following require explicit approval:

- Data residency commitments.
- Custom retention periods.
- Custom deletion workflows.
- Customer-specific audit logs.
- Customer-specific encryption representations.
- Custom access controls.
- Security questionnaires with binding commitments.
- Regulatory representations.
- Evidence production obligations.

No commercial actor may create security or retention obligations casually.

## NO-GO Conditions

A deal must be rejected, paused, or restructured when any of the following are present:

- The deal violates governance policy.
- The deal violates commercial hardening principles.
- The deal requires undocumented exceptions.
- The deal destroys the approved margin floor.
- The deal creates unbounded liability.
- The deal depends on false or unverified capability claims.
- The deal requires unsafe implementation practices.
- The deal weakens artifact retention.
- The deal creates unsupported customer-specific obligations.
- The deal creates obligations that cannot be audited.
- The deal introduces irreversible strategic precedent without approval.

Revenue does not override NO-GO conditions.

## Required Decision Record

Every Level 2 or Level 3 exception must have a decision record containing:

- Customer or opportunity identifier.
- Requested exception.
- Business rationale.
- Financial impact.
- Margin impact.
- Delivery impact.
- Support impact.
- Security, data, or retention impact.
- Approval owner.
- Expiration or review date.
- Final decision.
- Evidence link or repository reference.

An undocumented exception is not approved.

## Escalation Triggers

Escalation is mandatory when:

- Discount exceeds standard authority.
- Margin floor is threatened.
- Payment terms are non-standard.
- Contract terms create open-ended exposure.
- Delivery commitments require custom work.
- Security or data commitments are non-standard.
- Customer requests exclusivity or favored pricing.
- Implementation cost is unknown.
- Support cost is unknown.
- The deal creates precedent risk.
- The decision cannot be reversed cheaply.

## Enforcement

This matrix is enforced as a repository-governed commercial control.

Future changes to this matrix must be made through pull request review, validation checks, and immutable Git history.

Commercial authority must remain:

- Explicit.
- Auditable.
- Durable.
- Conservative under uncertainty.
- Aligned with real-money resilience.
- Compatible with long-term artifact retention.
- Safe for autonomous and human governance workflows.
